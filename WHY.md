# What is going on here

If you wandered in here as a developer, the architecture might throw you, and yeah, a fair amount of the code is rough. Most of it was written by an AI coding agent. Stick with me for a minute, because this is one of the more exciting things you'll come across.

## A bit of background

I have wanted to play games like RimWorld for as long as I can remember. I was the kid who wanted to play Dwarf Fortress so badly it hurt and who knew, flatly, that he never would. Games this deep were not built for someone who cannot see the screen, and nobody was going to rebuild them for the handful of us who wanted in.

That has changed. It is possible now, and it is happening. (Dwarf Fortress itself is probably still out of reach for me, since it is so data-heavy, and RimWorld scratches the same itch anyway, so I am content to let that one sit.)

RimWorld Access is my piece of it. It makes the game playable with a screen reader and a keyboard. Other people are doing the same thing for other games, and I have linked a few I respect below.

## What this is

A handful of blind gamers, tired of waiting, using coding agents to build accessibility into games that were never going to get it otherwise. Some of us have a software background, some have almost none. The tools are what closed the gap.

The important part, and the one that is easy to miss, is that this is not the AI running on its own. The agent writes the code. A person who understands accessibility sets the bar, and nothing ships until it feels right to play.

## On the AI of it all

I will say up front that I am critical of AI slop, and I know what this repo is and how it got made. But the part I actually want you to walk away with is this: this technology is exciting, it is genuinely fun to build with, and this project is my favorite evidence that it is not all bad.

People love to tear into AI-generated work, and when there is nobody behind the wheel, I understand the reaction. But that is not what is happening here. There is a human steering every inch of this mod, deciding what good feels like, cutting whatever sounds wrong through a screen reader, refusing to call something finished until it actually plays well. That is the difference, and when it is there, what comes out the other end is not slop. It is one of the best uses of these tools I have found.

I have been around this stuff for years, and part of why I am writing this down is to have something real I can show the AI people I know. Not a polished portfolio piece, but an honest example of what these tools can do in the hands of someone who actually knows what they want from them.

This file was generated too, though all of it started as my own notes or me talking at a microphone. I write slowly and don't enjoy it, and the hours I would spend polishing words are hours the mod does not improve, so I spend them on the mod instead.

What I care about is whether it runs and whether it gives a blind player something they could not do before. It does, and getting to watch that happen, including for myself, has been the most fun I have had with a hobby in years. The professional in me knows this repo is messy. The gamer in me could not care less, because I get to play RimWorld now, and so do plenty of people who never could. I am not planning to stop any time soon, unless the government finds a way to take all the models away like they took my sweet Fable. :(
